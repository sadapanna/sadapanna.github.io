Meet Ekka — the card game where every phone is a hand

A colour-and-number card game for two to eight players that runs in the browser. One person opens a room, everyone else types a four-letter code, and each phone becomes a private hand of cards. Free, nothing to install, no account, no ads.

The Sadapanna team
Published November 12, 2026

Most living rooms know this moment. Someone says "cards?" and the deck comes out of a drawer, a few cards short as always. Or it doesn't come out at all, because it was left at someone's house.

So the phones come out instead, and that goes wrong in a different way. One card app wants everybody to sign up first. Another plays an advert between rounds. A third only works if every person in the room installs the same thing, and by the time the last download finishes the mood has moved on.

We made Ekka for that moment, and we set ourselves one test: someone who has just heard the room code should be in the game within about a minute. Open a page, type four letters, pick up your cards.

The rules, in two minutes

Ekka is a shedding game: the first player to get rid of every card wins the round. If you have played UNO, you already know how to play, because the rules come from the same family.

The standard deck has 108 cards in four colours: red, yellow, green and blue. Each colour also carries its own shape (circle, triangle, square or diamond), so colour-blind players can tell them apart. Most cards are numbers, 0 to 9. On your turn you play one card that matches the top of the pile by colour, or by number or symbol. If nothing fits, you draw one, and if that card fits you may play it.

Then there are the cards that start arguments:

  • Hop skips the next player.
  • Flip reverses the direction of play.
  • +2 makes the next player draw two.
  • Wild lets you pick the colour.
  • Wild +4 lets you pick the colour, and the next player draws four.

One rule catches everybody at least once. When you are down to a single card you must hit Ekka!. If someone else taps Catch first, you draw two.

When a round ends, everyone's leftover cards are turned face up and counted. Numbers score their face value, action cards 20, wilds 50. The winner of the round collects the points.

Three ways to play

Before the first card goes down, the host picks a mode in the room.

♾️

Classic

Open-ended. Play round after round for as long as you like, and the scoreboard keeps a running total. Stacking is an optional house rule here: switch it on and you can answer a +2 or a Wild +4 with one of your own.

⏳

Timed

The host picks a 5, 10 or 15 minute match. Win a round and you collect the value of every card the others are still holding. When the clock runs out the round stops where it is, the lightest hand takes that last pot, and the highest total wins the match.

🔥

Ruthless

The harsh one. Draw cards always stack. If you cannot play, you keep drawing until you can. The deck gains Wild +6 and Hop all, which skips everyone so you go again. Anyone who ends up holding 15 cards is knocked out of the round.

One room, and every hand stays private

One person taps Create a room and gets a four-letter code, a QR code and an invite link. Everyone else joins from their own phone or laptop.

Your hand is on your screen and nobody else's. The table is shared: the pile, the deck, the turn order, and how many cards each person is holding. If you are short of people, the host can add computer players to fill the seats. There is also a Play the computer table for when it is just you.

It should feel like cards

A card game on a screen can end up feeling like a form with buttons. We wanted the opposite. Cards fly from the deck to hands and from hands to the pile. You can tap a card to play it, or flick it onto the pile. Slide a finger along a crowded hand and each card lifts in turn so you can read it. The table is dark, and its rim lights up in the colour in play. There are sound effects, and Hop, Flip, +2 and +4, Wild, the Ekka call, getting caught and winning each have an effect of their own.

A phone makes a good hand of cards. It's private, it's already in your pocket, and nobody has to shuffle.

A few smaller things sit around the table:

  • Avatars. Pick one before you sit down.
  • Reactions. Tap an emoji and it pops up at your seat on everyone's screen.
  • A turn timer. Each turn gets 30 seconds by default, and if it runs out you draw a card. The host can change the length or switch it off. It is separate from the match clock in Timed mode, which counts the whole match.

There is no game server

Sadapanna runs no server for Ekka. The host's browser runs the game, and the other devices connect to it directly using WebRTC, the technology browsers use for video calls. To find each other the devices need an introduction, and for that Ekka uses a public matchmaking service called PeerJS. It matches devices by room code and nothing more. Game data goes from device to device.

🔒

No accounts, nothing collected. The only things Ekka saves are your name, your avatar, your sound preference and a random id that lets you take your seat back after a refresh, and it saves them in your own browser. Same rule as everything we make: no data to sell, because we never collect any.

We have written before about how companies use the data they hold on you to set prices. Our answer is to hold none.

The honest limits

A game without a server has edges, and it is better to know them before game night.

  • It is most reliable when everyone is on the same wifi. It often works across different networks too, but some mobile networks block direct connections.
  • The room lives on the host's device. If the host refreshes, the room reopens. If the host closes the tab, the room ends.

The smaller mishaps are covered. If a guest's phone locks or drops off wifi, they rejoin automatically. If a player leaves, a computer player takes over their hand.

Quick answers

Is Ekka free, and do I need to install an app?

Ekka is free, with no ads and no account. It runs in the browser at sadapanna.com/app/ekka/, so there is nothing to install.

How many people can play?

Two to eight. The host can add computer players to fill empty seats, and there is a Play the computer table if you are on your own.

Do we all need to be on the same wifi?

No, but that is when it is most reliable. It often works across different networks too. Some mobile networks block direct connections between devices, and then a player may not be able to join.

Are there different game modes?

Three, and the host picks one before the game starts. Classic is open-ended with a running scoreboard. Timed is a 5, 10 or 15 minute match where the highest total wins. Ruthless makes draw cards stack, adds Wild +6 and Hop all, and knocks out anyone holding 15 cards.

Does it work on iPhone?

Ekka runs in modern mobile browsers, including Safari.
